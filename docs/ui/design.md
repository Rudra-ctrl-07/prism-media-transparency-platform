Master UI Design Prompt — PRISM (Competitive-Positioned)
Design a landing page and product UI for PRISM, a media transparency platform. The design must visually and functionally outperform two categories of competitor: cluttered rating-dashboards (Ground News, AllSides, NewsGuard, MBFC) and shallow single-verdict AI checkers (Auren, BSmeter, Neutral News AI). Every design choice below exists to close a specific gap those products leave open.
1. Beat the "static rating badge" problem (Ground News, AllSides, NewsGuard) These competitors show a bias/credibility label as a flat, unexplained badge — a color-coded pill with no visible reasoning behind it. PRISM's score UI must never be a dead badge. Every credibility score is an animated, filling arc/gauge that is clickable and expands into the reasoning behind it. The badge is a summary, never the destination — this alone visually signals "we show our work" versus their "trust our label."
2. Beat the "single AI verdict" problem (Auren, BSmeter, Neutral News AI) These tools produce one AI opinion with no visible disagreement or process. PRISM's core UI moment — the thing competitors cannot copy without rebuilding their entire backend — is the multi-agent debate visualization: three distinct analyst "voices" (Progressive / Conservative / Omission-Focused) shown as separate animated nodes that visibly argue via pulsing connector lines, converging into a Moderator verdict. This must be the single most prominent interactive element on the page, front and center in the live example section — not buried in a settings panel. No competitor currently visualizes disagreement between AI perspectives; make this impossible to miss.
3. Beat the "reference tool, not reading experience" problem (MBFC, Ad Fontes) These are static databases you have to search — no feed, no flow. PRISM must feel like a living, scrollable reading experience: a continuously updating Transparency Feed as the primary surface, not a lookup tool. Design the feed with the same editorial rhythm as a premium reading app (generous spacing, serif headlines, calm pacing) — never a cramped comparison table.
4. Beat the "Western/US-centric political binary" problem (flagged by reviewers against Ground News/AllSides/MBFC) Competitors reduce everything to a left-center-right slider, which reviewers explicitly call out as limiting for non-US stories. PRISM's bias visualization should avoid a single horizontal left-right bar; use a more flexible radial or multi-axis indicator that can represent bias dimensions beyond one political spectrum (e.g., framing, emotional language, omission — each its own small dimension) — visually communicating "we don't flatten every story into one American axis."
5. Beat the "dashboard clutter" aesthetic (World Monitor, indirect but sets the bar for "serious data tool" look) World Monitor wins on density; PRISM should never try to out-dashboard it. Where competitors show 10+ badges/metrics/toggles per story, PRISM shows one score, one debate, one clear verdict at a time — expensive-minimal, generous whitespace, near-monochrome with a single sparing teal accent, serif headlines, thin hairline dividers instead of bordered cards, subtle grain texture, custom cursor interactions. This restraint itself is the differentiator against every dashboard-style competitor.
6. Beat the "paywall frustration" complaint (repeatedly cited against Ground News) Reviewers consistently flag that Ground News's best features (Blindspot, factuality, ownership data) sit behind a paywall, frustrating free users. PRISM's free tier must visibly deliver the full transparency experience (classical ML score + source link + basic reasoning) with only depth/volume gated behind Pro (unlimited deep verifications, priority Claude-quality debate) — and the pricing section should make this distinction explicit and honest, not hide what's free vs. paid.
7. Motion and interaction rules


3D only in the hero (ambient node network) and a faint echo in the closing section — nowhere else

All functional elements (gauges, the agent-debate diagram, feed cards) are 2D, fast, legible

No dark patterns, no fake urgency — the honesty of the interaction design is itself competitive proof against tools that hide features behind paywalls or unexplained scores
Net positioning statement for the designer: every other tool in this space asks you to trust a label. PRISM is the only one that shows its reasoning, in motion, for free, without flattening the world into one political axis. The UI's entire job is to make that difference felt within the first ten seconds on the page.
